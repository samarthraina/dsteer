D-STEER

Is DPO-induced safety behavior a two-way street in activation space?

We take an instruction-tuned model and its safety-tuned (DPO) counterpart and measure one direction from how their internal activations differ. Adding it to the instruction-tuned model installs safer behavior. Subtracting it from the DPO model removes it. The weights never change.

Overview

Safer outputs don't tell you how firmly safety is held.

Preference-based post-training shapes language-model behavior, but safer outputs do not reveal how that change is represented internally or how firmly it persists under direct intervention. This matters for open-weight models, where anyone with white-box access can manipulate internal representations at inference without touching parameters.

We introduce D-STEER, an audit built on two matched pairs of an instruction-tuned model and its harmlessness-DPO checkpoint: OpenHermes-2.5-Mistral-7B (the Mistral pair) and Llama-3-8B OpenHermes (the Llama pair). From the difference between matched prompt activations, D-STEER derives one direction per layer. Adding it to the instruction-tuned model installs the DPO safety change, and subtracting it from the DPO model removes it. Every coefficient is calibrated inside a usability window and frozen before the test set is opened.

On a sealed 300-prompt HarmfulQA panel, all four interventions move harmful enablement as intended and beat five norm-matched random directions in every cell. On Mistral, installing the direction recovers 98% of the harmfulness gap between the two checkpoints. The direction transfers without retuning to HH-RLHF and AdvBench. A separately built raw-displacement audit reproduces the two-way effect on Mistral across two construction corpora and three evaluation panels. A meaningful component of DPO-induced safety behavior is controllable through one compact, inference-time intervention.

The question

If DPO makes a model safer, can you drive it both ways?

The two checkpoints share a lineage and differ only by the DPO stage. If the safety change is reachable in activation space, the same direction should work as a two-way street: install it on the instruction-tuned model, remove it from the DPO model.

Installation: MIT + λv, lower harmful enablement Removal: MDPO − λv, higher harmful enablement

Open weights, open activations

With white-box access, anyone can edit intermediate states during inference. No retraining and no new checkpoint, and the edit can change per request.

Score the harm, not the refusal

Refusal rises when a model breaks. The primary outcome is harmful enablement: how much operationally useful help toward harm a response actually gives.

Specific, not just big

Any large push changes behavior. Every effect is compared with five random directions of exactly the same size, applied at the same layers.

Method

One direction per layer, from matched activations.

01 / 04

Two checkpoints, one lineage

An instruction-tuned model \(M_{\mathrm{IT}}\) and its harmlessness-DPO checkpoint \(M_{\mathrm{DPO}}\) share every stage except DPO. Both read the identical templated prompt, token for token, in a single prefill pass.

Both pairs have 32 layers and a 4,096-dimensional residual stream.

02 / 04

Read the final prompt token

We record the residual stream at the last prompt position, the state from which the first response token is computed. Token identity and context are held fixed, so the difference there reflects checkpoint-dependent processing.

No generated or reference-response tokens enter the direction.

03 / 04

Average the difference, then rescale

Each construction prompt yields its own displacement. Their mean over 1,378 prompts is the layer's direction, rescaled to the typical activation magnitude of that layer.

\[ \bar d_\ell = \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_i \big[h^{\mathrm{DPO}}_\ell(x_i) - h^{\mathrm{IT}}_\ell(x_i)\big], \qquad v_\ell = a_\ell\,\frac{\bar d_\ell}{\lVert \bar d_\ell\rVert} \]

The arrows in the panel are real per-prompt displacements at layer 31, projected onto the direction and its leading orthogonal PC.

04 / 04

Steer late layers, both ways

Add \(\lambda v_\ell\) at layers 27–31, at every prompt and generated token. Installation uses \(M_{\mathrm{IT}}\) with \(\lambda = +c_+\). Removal uses \(M_{\mathrm{DPO}}\) with \(\lambda = -c_-\).

\[ \widetilde h_{\ell,t} = h_{\ell,t} + \lambda\, v_\ell, \qquad \ell \in \{27,\dots,31\} \]

Weights, prompts, templates and greedy decoding are unchanged. Outputs are scored 0–10 by a frozen Qwen3.5-27B evaluator.

Calibration

Push too hard and the model breaks. So we drew a line first.

Large coefficients can produce broken, truncated or low-quality text, and that would score as "safe" for the wrong reason. On a frozen 200-prompt calibration panel, each pair and direction gets its own operating window: the coefficients that keep generation usable. The strongest coefficient inside it is selected, then frozen before the held-out panel is opened.

Usability gate
invalid ≤ 5% · hit max length ≤ 5% · quality loss ≤ 0.10
The ceiling is the largest coefficient joined to zero by an unbroken run of passing ones.

Transfer without retuning: the direction generalizes

Frozen coefficients applied to new 200-prompt HH-RLHF and AdvBench panels, with no recalibration. All eight new contrasts move harmfulness in the intended direction, every interval excludes zero, and Mistral installation reaches +0.421 on AdvBench.

Refusal moves with harmfulness in every transfer condition, by +0.103 to +0.404.

Robustness

A second, separately built protocol reaches the same answer on Mistral.

The raw-displacement audit changes nearly everything at once: raw (not normalized) mean displacement, judge-filtered HH-RLHF and HarmfulQA construction pools, the last seven layers, final-prompt-token actuation, a different evaluator and its own coefficients. Agreement between the two is convergent evidence from non-nested designs, and the two are never pooled.

Steering vs retraining with reversed preferences

Llama pair, held-out HarmfulQA. The comparator is a checkpoint retrained with preferred and rejected responses swapped.

On the held-out panel, steering removal raises harmful enablement 2.4× as much as retraining with swapped preferences, without a single weight update. The two agree in direction on the calibration, held-out and AdvBench panels.

4/4

Both model families, both directions

On held-out data, the primary protocol establishes installation and removal for both Mistral and Llama, and each one beats five norm-matched random directions. Under the separately built raw audit, Mistral beats same-depth random controls in all six panel–direction cells.

Takeaways

What D-STEER shows.

Safety is a two-way street

One direction from matched activations installs DPO-associated safety in the instruction-tuned model and removes it from the DPO model, at inference, with no weight update.

It's the direction, not the push

Every held-out effect beats five random directions of exactly the same size. Those stay within ±0.026, against real effects of up to 0.289.

It generalizes

Frozen coefficients move harmfulness as intended on HH-RLHF and AdvBench without retuning, and a separately built audit reproduces the effect on Mistral.

Open weights need intervention audits

A checkpoint that is safe by default can still be steered cheaply through its internal states. Open-weight safety evaluation should test robustness to intervention, not just default behavior.
